# Skate Culture  (design profile: `skate-culture`)

**Era:** core 1975–1999; permanent revival cycles ever since

*Deck art + sticker bombing + zine grunge: asphalt darks under safety orange and hazard yellow, distressed textures, thick white sticker outlines, hand-marker and pop display type, collage energy (Screaming-Hand-era Santa Cruz, Thrasher).*

## Origins

1970s-90s California skate: Dogtown's spray-can Z, Jim Phillips' Screaming Hand for Santa Cruz (1985), Powell-Peralta skull iconography, Thrasher's flaming masthead, photocopied zines and the sticker toss. Graphics made to be shredded — they had to read at speed and look better scratched.

## Composition & space

Collage chaos with one focal scream: a single loud central graphic, everything else applied like stickers at random rotations. Edges bleed, registration is deliberately off, and empty space gets tagged. Neatness is suspicious.

## Proportion & measure

Outlines fat — 4-6% of the graphic's width — with hard offsets of 4-8px. Applied elements carry 2-8 degrees of rotation jitter. Display type fills 70%+ of whatever contains it. Nothing is centered unless it's mocking the idea of being centered.

## Color logic

Asphalt black base with safety orange, hazard yellow and blood red at full saturation — colors layered like screen-print passes, separated by fat white outlines, never blended. If two inks touch, it's with a hard trap line, not a fade.

## Light

Harsh direct flash (fisheye at dusk), fluorescent parking-garage flatness, xerox blowout. Light documents, it never flatters — if the shot looks lit by a professional, reshoot it with the flash your friend was holding.

## Typography

Loud hand-drawn display — marker, brush, blade — ALL CAPS, warped to fill whatever shape it's trapped in. Ransom-note mixing is legal. Legibility loses to attitude on every close call.

## Materiality & finish

Grip-tape grit, xerox noise, scraped ply, spray-stencil bleed, ripped paper, curb wax. Every surface is distressed by USE, not by a filter trying to look like use — wear patterns belong where wear would happen.

## Motion & rhythm

Abrupt punk cuts: jump cuts, frame-skips, shake on impact, marquees that scroll like a bearing rolling. VHS artifacts welcome. Smooth easing is for mall brands.

## Imagery

High-contrast black-and-white fisheye skate photography, hand illustration (skulls, hands, flames, eyeballs), photocopied halftones. Faces sneer; nothing poses.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Shouted slang and inside jokes — 'SK8 OR DIE' — loyal, self-deprecating, allergic to marketing-speak. The copy sounds hand-lettered even when typed, and it's talking to the crew, not to a demographic.

## Sound

Punk and thrash, lo-fi hip-hop, urethane wheels on concrete, the ka-chunk of a landed kickflip, tape hiss over everything. Production polish is treated as a betrayal.

## Where it lives

Deck bottoms and grip tape, sticker-bombed rails and lampposts, zines, shop walls, tour vans, curbs that have been waxed by three generations.

## Kinship (family tree)

Sibling of punk zines and graffiti; parent of modern streetwear graphics; cousin of grunge; the mall's other tribe and sworn opposite of `preppy-2000s`. Don't confuse with generic 'urban' styling — skate runs on specific brands, specific spots and in-jokes that gatekeep themselves.

## Hierarchy of power

Attitude > central graphic > texture > type > color. Color is a costume; attitude is the body wearing it. A technically perfect comp with no snarl is a failed comp.

## Canon (study these)

Study: Jim Phillips' Screaming Hand (Santa Cruz, 1985), VCJ's Ripper for Powell-Peralta, the Dogtown cross, Thrasher's flaming masthead, Toy Machine's devil, Spitfire's bighead, and any sticker-bombed lamppost.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Distress the display, never the body copy — captions stay clean on asphalt at AA+. Safety orange on black clears 7:1; don't stack orange text on yellow stickers. Light mode is the xerox: near-white paper, black ink, one red accent — the zine was always light mode.

## Signature moves

1. One screaming central graphic; everything else is stickers
2. Fat white outline + hard 4px offset shadow on any element that matters
3. Rotate every applied element 2–8 degrees — nothing sits straight
4. Finish with a grain/xerox pass over the whole composition

- **gloss**: gritty-flat: heavy grain over everything; sticker elements get a fat white outline + hard offset shadow (4px 4px 0), never a soft blur

- **patterns**: noise-texture, diagonal-stripes
- **techniques (fx)**: grain-overlay, stroke-text, pixel-art-shadow, infinite-marquee

**Rules:** DO rotate stickers a few degrees, distress edges, clash orange on black. DON'T center things neatly or use thin elegant type — it should look hand-applied.

**You've lost the plot when:** Instant poser-merch when polish arrives: soft shadows, gradients, symmetric layouts, corporate-clean type, or distress applied evenly everywhere like a texture swatch.
