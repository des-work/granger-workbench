# Art Deco  (design profile: `art-deco`)

**Era:** 1925–1940 machine-age zenith; revived for luxury ever since

*1920s-40s controlled opulence: black + gold + jewel tones, graduated sunbursts (rays tighter at the center), stepped ziggurat frames, fans, chevrons, strict symmetry, and tall geometric letterforms with uniform strokes.*

## Origins

Named for the 1925 Paris Exposition des Arts Décoratifs: machine-age optimism wearing jewelry. Cassandre's travel posters, the Chrysler Building's crown (1930), Erté's figures, ocean-liner ballrooms and Gatsby's parties — geometry, luxury and speed fused into one discipline.

## Composition & space

Strict bilateral symmetry with vertical thrust: compositions ASCEND in stepped tiers like a ziggurat. Frames nest within frames; ornament concentrates at borders and crowns while centers stay calm. Everything either radiates from a point or climbs toward one.

## Proportion & measure

Strong verticality: display lockups at 2:1 height-to-width or taller. Stepped frames in odd tier counts (3, 5, 7), each step roughly halving. Sunbursts carry 9-15 rays with graduated spacing. Borders travel in thick-thin pairs — a 5px line escorted by a 1px line.

## Color logic

Black lacquer is the ground and gold is the protagonist. Exactly one jewel tone guests per composition — emerald OR sapphire OR ruby, never a chorus. Cream provides the breathing room. High contrast, zero pastels, no earth tones — this is a nighttime aesthetic.

## Light

Warm key light on polished brass — reflections drawn as controlled lines along edges, never scattered sparkle. Marquee bulbs, candlelight on lacquer; light forms RAYS and edges, not pools.

## Typography

Tall, condensed, geometric letterforms with uniform strokes and high waistlines, set as letter-spaced caps (Poiret One; Cinzel for engraved moments). Numerals are treated as jewelry. Nothing bubbly, nothing distressed, nothing lowercase-casual.

## Materiality & finish

Polished brass and gold leaf, black lacquer, macassar ebony, frosted glass, chrome trim. Surfaces are hard and reflective but CONTROLLED — an even sheen, never glitter or sparkle. Metal reads as machined, not molten.

## Motion & rhythm

Mechanical elegance on clockwork timing: rays sweep outward, elements rise step by step, reveals fan open like a peacock or a hand of cards. Precise starts and stops — bounce and overshoot are vulgarities.

## Imagery

Stylized streamlined illustration in the Cassandre manner — simplified planes, dramatic diagonals — or architectural photography shot looking UP. People appear as elongated elegant silhouettes, never candid.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Title-case proclamations with period confidence — 'The Future Arrives Nightly.' Formal, theatrical, a little grand; superlatives are permitted because the symmetry earned them.

## Sound

Big-band brass, a clarinet glissando, champagne fizz, the chime of a brass elevator. Tempo strict and metronomic — the music, like the geometry, does not rubato.

## Where it lives

Hotel lobbies and theatre marquees, liquor and perfume packaging, gala invitations, film title sequences, penthouse bars — places where evening is the native hour.

## Kinship (family tree)

Child of cubism and the machine age; sibling of `golden-archive` (both gold-on-dark — the archive is cosmic and quiet, deco is urban and theatrical); opposite of `organic-tech` (geometry vs biology). Don't confuse with art nouveau — nouveau curves like a vine, deco steps like a skyscraper.

## Hierarchy of power

Symmetry > gold/black contrast > typography > ornament > the jewel accent. Break the symmetry and nothing downstream can save you; keep it and even sparse ornament reads as opulence.

## Canon (study these)

Study: Cassandre's Normandie poster, the Chrysler Building's crown, Erté's Symphony in Black, Hildreth Meière's Radio City plaques, Gatsby title treatments, and any 1930 cinema marquee photographed at night.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Gold on near-black clears 7:1 — AAA at display sizes; emerald is for large elements only. Daytime deco exists: cream field, black geometry, gold accents (think deco menus and invitations). Never dim gold with opacity — mix it toward bronze so it stays metal, not ghost.

## Signature moves

1. Frame the subject in a symmetric stepped (ziggurat) border
2. Graduated sunburst behind the title — rays tighter at the center
3. Letter-spaced condensed caps; treat numerals as jewelry
4. Black field, gold protagonist, exactly one emerald guest

**Rules:** DO center and mirror everything, graduate ray spacing, pair gold with one jewel tone. DON'T use organic shapes or asymmetry — geometry is the glamour.

**You've lost the plot when:** Undone by asymmetry, organic squiggles, more than one jewel tone at once, or soft rounded type. Deco is discipline wearing jewelry — remove the discipline and it's a casino carpet.
