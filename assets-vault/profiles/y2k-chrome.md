# Y2K Chrome  (design profile: `y2k-chrome`)

**Era:** c. 1997–2003 — the millennium countdown and its morning after

*1997-2003 millennium futurism: liquid chrome, silver everything, translucent candy plastic, lens flares and blob forms floating in white voids — the future staged as a showroom you were about to enter.*

## Origins

Designed in the countdown to 2000: the iMac G3's translucent candy shells, The Designers Republic and Wipeout's identities, TDK/MiniDisc ads, inflatable furniture, early CGI metaballs. Optimism with a metallic taste — everyone was about to live in the future, and the future was silver.

## Composition & space

A centered hero object floats in a white or gradient void; orbit rings and elliptical arcs circle it; a swoosh crosses behind. Generous emptiness is the point — the future is uncluttered because everything old was thrown away.

## Proportion & measure

Blobs bulge past geometry — superellipse corners, radius beyond 50%. Chrome bevels stay thin (1-2% of the object). Flare stars carry 4-8 points; orbit rings tilt 15-30 degrees. Type stays small relative to the hero object — the object is the message.

## Color logic

Silver-to-white chrome gradients, ice white, electric blue — with translucent candy (blueberry, lime, grape) as accents. Black is rare. Color exists mostly to make the chrome look colder and the plastic look edible.

## Light

Infinite studio: white-cyc lighting with hard speculars, reflections of a showroom that doesn't exist, and a lens flare earned by every bright edge. Light is an effect, never a mood.

## Typography

Techno grotesques and squared faces (Orbitron energy), lowercase futurism or widely spaced caps, often set on a curve or riding an orbit ring. Digital-display faces appear as accents. Serifs do not exist in this future.

## Materiality & finish

Liquid mercury, polycarbonate, brushed aluminum, holographic foil, inflatable PVC. Everything is hard, cool and reflective — matte is forbidden; even shadows look polished.

## Motion & rhythm

Metaballs morph, orbits rotate slowly, flares sweep on cue; the camera moves like a product turntable. Easing is sleek and continuous — mechanical confidence, no bounce, no jitter.

## Imagery

3D-rendered hero products floating mid-air, silver humanoids, wireframe globes, macro shots of translucent plastic. Humans appear reflected in chrome, rarely in person.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Millennium-promise speak: 'welcome to tomorrow.' Product neologisms with internal caps (iThing, eEverything), confident and slightly alien — a voice assembled in a clean room, cool to the touch.

## Sound

Trance and big-beat, TR-909 sheen, the ghost of a modem handshake, the Intel bong. UI sounds are metallic ticks and airlock whooshes — the room tone of a server that loves you.

## Where it lives

Gadget packaging, CD-ROM intros, music-channel idents, car-show concept stands, sneaker-tech ads, winamp-skin desktops — anywhere the millennium was being sold in advance.

## Kinship (family tree)

Cousin of `frutiger-aero` — the same optimism one era earlier: Y2K chromes it, Aero hydrates it. Vaporwave samples THIS material ironically twenty years later. Shares hardware with `cyberpunk` but not weather: Y2K is showroom-clean, cyberpunk is street-dirty.

## Hierarchy of power

Material (chrome) > hero object > flare and light > motion > type. If the chrome convinces, everything else is garnish; if it doesn't, nothing survives.

## Canon (study these)

Study: the iMac G3 and clamshell iBook, The Designers Republic's Wipeout 3 identity, TDK MiniDisc ads, the Air Max 97, Windows Media Player skin culture, Björk's 'All Is Full of Love' video.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Electric blue clears 4.5:1 on the ice field — body-safe; chrome gradients are for objects, never behind text (legibility dies on mirrors). Dark mode is 'showroom night': graphite field, chrome stays bright, blue glows. Reduced motion: freeze the orbits, keep the flare static.

## Signature moves

1. One chrome blob hero centered in a white void
2. Tilted elliptical orbit ring passing behind the subject
3. A four-point lens flare on the brightest edge
4. Type on a curve: lowercase, letter-spaced, slightly alien

**Rules:** DO center the hero, tilt the orbit, earn the flare. DON'T add warmth, texture or clutter — the void is the luxury; one object floating beats five arranged.

**You've lost the plot when:** Dies of warmth: wood, paper grain, hand-drawn anything, or matte surfaces. Also dies of clutter — two hero objects is zero hero objects. The future has room for exactly one thing at a time.
