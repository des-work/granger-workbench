# Avant Basic  (design profile: `avant-basic`)

**Era:** c. 2019–2023 — peak Instagram/TikTok interiors and DTC e-commerce

*2019-22 Instagram/Shein-era maximalism reviving Memphis and the 60s-70s: warped checkerboards, squiggles, solid daisies, diamond stars and arch 'window' shapes in bright retro pastels + hot pink and cobalt. Playful, clashing, flat.*

## Origins

A direct revolt against 2010s millennial minimalism (beige rooms, thin sans-serifs, tasteful sameness). It resurrects the Memphis Group (Sottsass, 1981) and 60s-70s psychedelia through e-commerce and Instagram interiors — wavy mirrors, checkered rugs, squiggle candles — where being photographed well matters more than being restrained.

## Composition & space

Dense and busy on purpose: every zone hosts a motif, elements overlap like stickers, and the same shape repeats at several scales. Grids exist to be warped — checkerboards bend and perspective-melt. Balance is achieved by color weight, not symmetry; nothing is centered out of politeness.

## Proportion & measure

Chunky everything: stroke weights at least 6% of the element's width (marker-line minimum), radii pill-round, motifs repeated at three scales stepping roughly 2.5x. Spacing runs tight — about half of what a minimalist would leave — because emptiness reads as timidity here.

## Color logic

Bright retro pastels held at near-equal saturation — lilac, butter yellow, tangerine, lime, cobalt, hot pink — with no neutral doing rescue work. Adjacent colors are chosen to vibrate (pink beside red, lime beside lilac). Fields meet at hard edges; a gradient would be an apology.

## Light

Flat, even, shadowless — lit like product photography on a colored seamless. Color does all the modeling; light is not allowed to editorialize. In rooms: bright daylight bouncing off lacquered color, no moody corners anywhere.

## Typography

Chubby and bulbous: 70s bubble-serif display (Cooper Black energy — Shrikhand) set wide and generous, often bending along an arch or wave. Body text stays in a simple rounded sans so the display face can be the loudest object in the room.

## Materiality & finish

Flat matte, like screen-printed vinyl or glazed ceramic. In objects: lacquered wavy mirrors, tufted rugs, checkered tile. There is no gloss, no drop shadow, no depth — dimension comes from overlap and outline, never light.

## Motion & rhythm

Cartoon physics: squash-and-stretch, wobble, wiggle. Squiggles undulate, daisies spin lazily, stickers land with a bounce and a few degrees of rotation jitter. Easing is playful overshoot, never linear.

## Imagery

Flat vector illustration over photography. When photos appear they are staged still-lifes on colored seamless backdrops — objects arranged like candy, lit flat, no moody shadows.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Playful lowercase, pun-forward, chatty — 'hi, we make cute stuff.' Copy sounds like a friend captioning a photo, never a brand issuing a statement. Emoji-adjacent energy without leaning on emoji; formality is the only banned word.

## Sound

Bubbly plucks, marimba pop, bedroom indie-pop; interface blips as rounded as the shapes. Nothing aggressive, nothing epic — the soundtrack of a nice afternoon with snacks.

## Where it lives

Creator studios and Gen-Z apartments (the wavy mirror, the checkered rug, the squiggle candle), DTC packaging, nail salons, sticker sheets, phone cases, tote bags — anywhere a photo might be taken of the thing.

## Kinship (family tree)

Direct child of Memphis (1981) and 60s-70s psychedelia; cousin of vaporwave (shares the nostalgia, rejects the digital chrome); sworn enemy of millennial minimalism. Don't confuse with `pastel` — pastel is calm, avant is loud in pastel's clothing.

## Hierarchy of power

Motif > color clash > display type > layout. The pattern IS the message; body copy is a polite guest who gets a clean white card to stand on and no more.

## Canon (study these)

Study: the Memphis Group's Carlton bookcase (Sottsass, 1981), Camille Walala's murals, Lisa Says Gah lookbooks, the c. 2021 wavy-mirror/checkered-rug Instagram interior, squiggle candles and Poster Club prints.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Pattern never sits behind body copy — text floats on cream/white cards above the chaos. Hot pink fails contrast on lilac, so running text falls back to deep plum. Dark mode: swap the cream field for deep plum and keep motifs at FULL saturation — it becomes avant-after-dark, never gray.

## Signature moves

1. Warp a checkerboard and put it behind the subject
2. Set the title in a bubble serif riding an arch
3. Scatter daisies + diamond stars at three different scales
4. Pick two clashing pastels and let them touch with a hard edge

## Components (this vault)

- **tokens/style**: `avant-basic` (`assets kit avant-basic` for a full stylesheet)
- **palette**: `avant-play` — #f6e7ee #d81b7a #5438dc #f3a712 #7fc243 #3a2038 #fffaf5
- **fonts**: display: Shrikhand, body: Fredoka
- **textures (native)**: halftone, stripes
- **gloss**: flat-pop: hard flat fills, zero gel/glass; depth comes from overlapping shapes and offset outlines, not shadows
- **shapes**: squiggle, daisy, checker-wave, diamond-star, arch-window
- **patterns**: checkerboard
- **techniques (fx)**: blob-morph, stroke-text, pixel-art-shadow

**Rules:** DO warp/liquify grids, mismatch colors on purpose, repeat one motif big. DON'T add gloss, gradients or drop shadows — flatness is the point.

**You've lost the plot when:** You've lost it when the palette starts matching tastefully, when a gradient or soft shadow sneaks in, or when the type turns thin and elegant — the whole point is confident, flat, deliberate clash.
