# Art Nouveau / Botanical Line  (design profile: `art-nouveau`)

**Era:** c. 1883–1914; contemporary botanical-poster interpretation

*A botanical print grown into a page: sinuous stems, elongated lettering, warm paper and a continuous ornamental frame.*

## Origins

An international movement across architecture, applied arts and graphic design. Natural forms and the whiplash line link the page to ironwork, stained glass, textiles and turn-of-the-century posters.

## Composition & space

A vertical framed poster. One botanical plate rises through the centre; the headline, curved border and folio belong to the same contour system.

## Proportion & measure

Long vertical proportions and an organic continuous frame. Keep the reading column narrow and the decorative border outside the text.

## Color logic

Warm lithographic stock, deep botanical green, dried terracotta and muted brass. Flat colour fields keep the print character.

## Light

Even paper light. Depth comes from overlapping contour lines, never glass or a digital glow.

## Typography

Fraunces for expressive serif titles, Lora for reading. The local fonts are practical modern companions, not claims to be historical Art Nouveau typefaces.

## Materiality & finish

Lithographic paper, botanical ink, stained-glass partitions and curved metalwork translated into SVG line.

## Motion & rhythm

A slow clipped botanical reveal if used; the complete illustration remains present with reduced motion.

## Imagery

Original iris stems and whiplash curves. Use licensed botanical prints and owned photographs when extending the collection.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Poetic but specific: an exhibition title, a botanical name, a season. Keep practical information direct and let one lyrical line carry the atmosphere.

## Sound

Quiet by default; no automatic audio. If a project needs sound, make the action explicit and keep the reading space free from continuous effects.

## Where it lives

Botanical exhibitions, cultural events, perfumery, book arts and decorative-arts collections.

## Kinship (family tree)

Art Deco is angular, stepped and geometric; this profile grows from organic curves. Organic Tech is functional and contemporary rather than a framed print.

## Hierarchy of power

Continuous botanical line > image silhouette > lettering > limited print colour.

## Canon (study these)

V&A whiplash examples, Guimard ironwork, Horta interiors and Mucha poster composition; study rather than copy their artworks.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Text sits on an opaque paper plane. Borders simplify on phones and never crowd the reading column.

## Signature moves

1. Let one organic line frame the composition
2. Extend botanical stems into the continuous page border
3. Keep colour flat and botanical
4. Protect a quiet opaque text field

**Rules:** DO connect ornament to the page structure. DO NOT use a stepped Deco sunburst, generic floating blobs or a modern glass card.

**You've lost the plot when:** If the border could belong to Art Deco without changing anything, the organic line has been lost.
