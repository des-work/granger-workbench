# Swiss International  (design profile: `swiss-international`)

**Era:** 1950s Basel/Zürich; the global default through the 70s–80s; permanent

*The International Typographic Style: objective white space, a mathematical grid, one neo-grotesque family, one signal red. Design as civic infrastructure — it informs perfectly and refuses, on principle, to decorate.*

## Origins

1950s Basel and Zürich: Müller-Brockmann's concert posters, Max Bill's concrete art, Emil Ruder's typography course — Bauhaus and De Stijl distilled into a teachable system that became the world's default 'good design' (airports, corporate identity, the NASA manual).

## Composition & space

The grid is sacred: modular columns, flush-left rag-right, asymmetric balance computed rather than felt. White space is structural, not leftover. Elements align across the entire system or they don't ship.

## Proportion & measure

Grid modules in strict ratios (thirds, fifths); two or three type sizes per piece with unmistakable jumps; rules exactly one point. The red element occupies under 10% of the area and collects 90% of the attention — that asymmetry is the design.

## Color logic

White field, black ink, ONE signal red. Photography stays black-and-white. Color is information, never decoration: if red appears twice, it means two things — and if it doesn't mean two things, delete one.

## Light

None. The page is a shadowless printed surface; any simulated depth betrays the flat honest plane. What light exists lives inside the documentary photography, which brings its own grey daylight.

## Typography

A neo-grotesque (Helvetica/Univers lineage — Inter here) in few weights, flush left, with tight even 'color' of the text block. Hierarchy by size, weight and position only. Tabular numerals; italics only when bold cannot do the job.

## Materiality & finish

Smooth offset paper, silkscreen ink, enamel signage. The material is PRINT itself — flatness as integrity. A texture would be a confession that the layout couldn't carry the page.

## Motion & rhythm

If it must move: strict translations along grid axes, hard cuts or metric easing, type never distorting. The model is a station departure board updating — precise, legible, indifferent to delight.

## Imagery

Objective black-and-white photography — documentary, unstyled, unsentimental — or none. Diagrams and pictograms outrank illustration. The map, the timetable and the poster are the native genres.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Informational, verb-lean, verifiable: 'Zürich 19:04, Gleis 7.' No adjective survives unless it can be measured. The designer is invisible; the message is everything; exclamation points do not exist.

## Sound

Station room tone: an announcement, a clock tick, footsteps on terrazzo. If music, metronome-precise minimalism. Interface sounds are single dry clicks — confirmation, not celebration.

## Where it lives

Transit systems and wayfinding, museums, government forms, corporate identity manuals, pharmaceutical packaging — and every tech company that wants to look neutral and trustworthy.

## Kinship (family tree)

Ancestor of `mono` (Swiss gone brutalist-dark) and of `neutral`; `hi-contrast` is Swiss stripped to pure accessibility signal. Sworn opposite of `avant-basic` — the grid versus the warp. Don't confuse with lifestyle minimalism: Swiss is dense with information, empty only of decoration.

## Hierarchy of power

Grid > typography > white space > the red element > photography. Everything else was already deleted before the first draft.

## Canon (study these)

Study: Müller-Brockmann's Beethoven poster, the NASA Graphics Standards Manual, Vignelli's NYC subway map and the SBB rail identity, Ruder's 'Typographie', the 'Helvetica' documentary.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Black on white is 21:1; the signal red clears 5:1 for text but should stay a signal. Dark mode is the departure board — black field, white type, red signals — already canonical. Because the grid is ratio-based it survives any size, medium or century unchanged.

## Signature moves

1. Build the grid first; let it show in ruthless alignment
2. Two sizes of one grotesque, flush left, and nothing else
3. Place ONE red element exactly where the eye should land
4. Replace every decoration you were about to add with white space

## Components (this vault)

- **tokens/style**: `swiss-international` (`assets kit swiss-international` for a full stylesheet)
- **palette**: `swiss-signal` — #ffffff #f2f2f2 #d9d9d9 #595959 #111111 #d0021b #0a4d8c
- **fonts**: display: Inter, body: Inter
- **textures (native)**: paper
- **gloss**: zero — matte print forever; even the red is flat ink. A gradient here is not a choice, it is a scandal
- **shapes**: grid-marks, signal-dot, crossbar-arrow
- **patterns**: grid, graph-paper
- **techniques (fx)**: subgrid-align, text-balance-wrap, container-query

**Rules:** DO build the grid first and let the alignments show; set flush left; earn every element. DON'T center sentimentally, decorate, or use two accent colors — one red, one meaning.

**You've lost the plot when:** Fails by ornament, by centering everything politely, by a second accent color, or by breaking the grid 'just this once.' Also fails by cosplay: Swiss without real information design is just Helvetica on a poster.
