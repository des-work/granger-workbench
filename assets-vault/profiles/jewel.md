# Jewel  (design profile: `jewel`)

**Era:** pigment economics from antiquity; a continuous luxury register since

*Deep saturated colour over dark grounds: emerald, sapphire, garnet and old gold, lit like a display case. Luxurious without glitter — the richness comes from pigment depth rather than from shine.*

## Origins

A colourist's position rather than a movement. Jewel tones are shades: a hue mixed toward black, which deepens it while reducing chroma, so they stay saturated without becoming bright. The cultural weight is older — Tyrian purple was ruinously expensive in antiquity, which is precisely why Roman and Byzantine emperors wore it, and deep colour has signalled cost ever since. Stained glass, lacquer, enamel and velvet all trade on the same effect.

## Composition & space

Formal and generously margined, with a clear centre of attention — a composition that behaves like a vitrine. Symmetry is used where it adds ceremony; elements are given space around them the way an object in a case is, so that isolation reads as value.

## Proportion & measure

A 1.414 scale over four steps at a 17px base, 66ch measure, 28px gutters. Margins are wide and deliberate. Metallic rules stay hairline — thick gold is costume, thin gold is jewellery, and the difference is about one pixel.

## Color logic

Deep green, deep blue and oxblood over near-black, with aged brass as the only metallic. Every hue is a shade rather than a tint — darkened, never greyed — so the colour stays rich at low lightness. Two accents at most, and gold counts as one of them.

## Light

Directional and warm, like a single spot in a dark room: one side of an object lit, the other falling into the ground. Highlights are small and specular. The whole image sits dark so the lit parts can be the subject.

## Typography

A high-contrast serif with real thick-thin modulation (Playfair Display) over a warm text face (Fraunces), with small caps for labels. The serif's contrast is doing the same job as the palette — depth through range rather than through weight.

## Materiality & finish

Velvet, lacquer, enamel, stained glass, polished stone, aged brass. Surfaces are deep rather than shiny: the reference is light going into a material and coming back changed, which is what makes a gemstone look different from a sequin.

## Motion & rhythm

Slow and weighted — 400ms on a long settle, small travel, a sheen that passes rather than flashes. Everything moves as though it has mass. Nothing is quick, because haste reads as cheap in this register.

## Imagery

Dark-ground still life, chiaroscuro product photography, textiles and stones shot close, architectural detail in low light. The background is usually darker than the subject and often falls off to black entirely.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Measured and confident, slightly formal, never hurried. Short declarative sentences with no superlatives — the copy assumes the quality is evident and does not argue for it. Urgency and discounting both break the register instantly.

## Sound

Low strings, a struck singing bowl, the soft close of a well-made box. Interface sounds are warm, low and brief — the audio equivalent of weight.

## Where it lives

Luxury retail and jewellery, spirits and fine dining, premium hospitality, private banking and members' services, theatre and opera, and any product whose argument is that it costs more for a reason.

## Kinship (family tree)

Shares `golden-archive`'s dark ground and metallic, though the archive is cosmic and institutional where this is material and commercial. Cousin of `dark-academia` through the serif and the low light, minus the clutter and the melancholy. `art-deco` is this palette given geometry. Don't confuse with `cyberpunk` dark: nothing here emits.

## Hierarchy of power

Depth of colour > directional light > the serif's contrast > space around objects > the metallic. If the colours are bright rather than deep, no amount of gold rule will buy back the richness.

## Canon (study these)

Study: Dutch golden-age still life, stained glass at Chartres, Cartier and Asprey's identities, Fortuny and Venetian velvets, and the colour plates in any good book on lacquer.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Cream on navy runs 13:1 and reversed text takes the extra leading it needs. Brass is display-only at around 4:1 — a gold caption is a legibility bug wearing a nice suit. A light mode exists as cream grounds with jewel accents, though it loses the vitrine effect and becomes a different, gentler style.

## Signature moves

1. Shade every hue toward black, never toward grey
2. Light one side only and let the rest fall to the ground
3. Keep the metallic to hairline rules and small marks
4. Give the hero object vitrine space and nothing else nearby

## Components (this vault)

- **tokens/style**: `jewel` (`assets kit jewel` for a full stylesheet)
- **palette**: `jewel-royal` — #14213d #1b2a4a #5f0f40 #0f766e #7c3aed #d4af37 #f8f4e8
- **fonts**: display: Playfair Display, body: Fraunces
- **textures (native)**: grain, brushed
- **gloss**: moderate and deep — lacquer rather than chrome. Light should look as though it entered the surface and returned slightly coloured, so highlights are small, warm and few. A broad white specular reads as plastic and undoes the whole material claim.
- **shapes**: gold-disk, stepped-frame, laurel-corner
- **patterns**: noise-texture, topographic
- **techniques (fx)**: spotlight-hover, sheen-sweep, gradient-border-mask, emboss-letterpress

**Rules:** DO darken hues rather than greying them, light from one direction, and keep the metallic hairline. DON'T brighten, don't add glitter, and don't let gold carry body text — depth is the effect, not sparkle.

**You've lost the plot when:** Fails by brightness and by glitter — a saturated bright green with a gold gradient is a casino, not a jeweller. Fails also by over-application: the richness depends on restraint and darkness, so a page where everything is deep and gold has no hierarchy left at all.
