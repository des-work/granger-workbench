# Organic Tech  (design profile: `organic-tech`)

**Era:** c. 2020–present — wellness tech and post-minimal product design

*Biomorphic + biophilic: shapes that read as cells, water or pebbles; the 'living earth' palette (sage, moss, clay, sand); gradients that shift *within* shapes; no harsh corners anywhere; spring-physics motion. Nature wired to a device.*

## Origins

Biomorphism (Arp's reliefs, Noguchi's tables) crossed with biophilic-design research — the finding that organic pattern measurably calms — arriving in 2020s product design as wellness tech, solarpunk optimism and the soft rebuttal to cold techno-minimalism. Nature is the interface metaphor.

## Composition & space

Generous negative space, but soft: content sits in rounded pools rather than boxes, and cards read as pebbles or cells scattered in loose organic clusters. The grid is felt, never seen; boundaries dissolve into the field instead of ending at a border.

## Proportion & measure

Radii at least 25% of element height — the pebble ratio — and clusters arranged in loose golden-ish asymmetry. Line weights approach zero: separation comes from ~8% value steps, not strokes. Whitespace holds 40%+ of the field, and the type scale climbs gently (1.25x), never leaps.

## Color logic

Low-saturation earth — a sage field with moss and clay used sparingly, the way plants sit in a room. Whites are warm (bone, oat), never clinical pure white. Contrast is carried by VALUE, not hue; nothing fluoresces. Gradients shift gently within a shape, like light through a leaf.

## Light

Diffused daylight through frosted glass: soft shadows with wide penumbras, golden-hour warmth welcome, hard specular points banned. Light behaves like it passed through a leaf or a paper shade on the way in.

## Typography

Humanist or geometric-rounded sans with low stroke contrast, roomy line-height and sentence case — reading should feel like a calm voice. No all-caps shouting; emphasis comes from weight and space, not size explosions.

## Materiality & finish

Matte ceramic, unglazed clay, frosted glass, linen, river stone. Grain is fine and organic. Light DIFFUSES rather than reflects — a frosted glow, never a wet highlight or chrome edge.

## Motion & rhythm

Spring physics everywhere: elements settle rather than snap, blobs morph continuously, and idle surfaces breathe (slow 2-4% scale oscillation). Nothing moves in a straight line at constant speed — that reads as machine, not organism.

## Imagery

Macro nature — leaf venation, water meniscus, stone texture — in soft daylight with shallow depth of field. Products photographed on stone, linen or oak; hands appear often; screens appear rarely.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Calm and low-ego, gentle second person — 'take a moment.' Science softened by nature metaphor; short sentences with room to breathe. It never urges, never counts down, never uses an exclamation point.

## Sound

Ambient pads, field recordings — water over stone, leaves — and soft haptic thumps. A notification should sound like a pebble dropped in a pond, not a bell demanding anything.

## Where it lives

Wellness and sleep apps, smart-home objects in oak-and-linen rooms, spa and skincare branding, sustainable packaging, biotech that wants to feel kind instead of clinical.

## Kinship (family tree)

Heir to biomorphism (Arp, Noguchi) and biophilic architecture; sibling of `pastel` (shares the softness, trades sugar for earth); natural complement of `hi-contrast`. Don't confuse with farmhouse-rustic — this is precise softness, engineered like a lens, not distressed like a barn door.

## Hierarchy of power

Shape language > palette calm > motion feel > type. If the corners are right, half the work is done; if the motion springs, the other half follows.

## Canon (study these)

Study: Noguchi's coffee table, Arp's reliefs, Headspace's blob era, Calm and Oura's branding, Karim Rashid's 'blobject' years (the Garbo can), and solarpunk concept art where circuitry grows leaves.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Sage and moss hover near 3:1 — reserve them for large elements and keep running text in deep loam ink. Dark mode is a nighttime greenhouse: deep moss field, sage as the light accent, whites kept warm. Reduced-motion users get slow crossfades — remove the springs, never the rounding.

## Signature moves

1. Replace every rectangle with a pebble or cell blob
2. One sage field, one clay accent, warm off-whites — stop there
3. Give every element spring-settle motion; let idle things breathe
4. Add fine grain so flat fields feel like paper, not pixels

**Rules:** DO round every corner (radius 20px+), blend layers, animate with springs. DON'T use straight lines, hard borders or neon — calm is the material.

**You've lost the plot when:** Dead the moment a hard corner, hairline border, neon accent or pure black arrives — or when motion turns linear and mechanical. If it feels like a lab instead of a greenhouse, back up.
