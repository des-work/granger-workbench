# Bauhaus / Workshop Modernism  (design profile: `bauhaus`)

**Era:** 1919–1933; contemporary workshop interpretation

*A workshop, not a colour theme: asymmetric typography, measured geometric studies and useful objects. Form, material and making occupy the same page.*

## Origins

The Bauhaus combined art and craft with industrial production. This profile draws on workshop pedagogy, typography and material studies rather than treating primary-colour shapes as the whole movement.

## Composition & space

Asymmetric poster with a huge workshop number, a colour-and-form study, orthogonal rules and a functional course index. Type shares the geometry of the objects.

## Proportion & measure

A clear modular grid; broad margins; a display headline several steps above the text. Geometric elements act as compositional masses, not scattered confetti.

## Color logic

Warm stock and black structure; limited red, blue and ochre areas. These are one documented study palette, not a claim that all Bauhaus work used primaries.

## Light

Flat print light; no emissive effects, glass, atmospheric bloom or floating shadows.

## Typography

Inter for practical text and Sora for geometric display; asymmetric flush-left setting, upright type and visible numerals.

## Materiality & finish

Paper, ink, steel, wood and woven studies. The web interpretation keeps the flatness of a printed workshop sheet.

## Motion & rhythm

Brief geometric translation or a direct state change. Never use animation as surface decoration.

## Imagery

Original geometric and material studies; object diagrams and owned workshop photographs.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Concise, functional and concrete: workshop, material, exercise, edition. Name the useful action before adding a poetic description.

## Sound

Quiet by default; no automatic audio. If a project needs sound, make the action explicit and keep the reading space free from continuous effects.

## Where it lives

Design studios, practical courses, architecture, furniture and cultural programmes where the relation between an object, its material and its construction matters.

## Kinship (family tree)

Shares systematic alignment with Swiss International, but lets geometric experiments and object studies lead. Memphis / Avant Basic is a later postmodern reaction, with decorative collisions instead of this functional order.

## Hierarchy of power

Composition and type > geometric form > useful information > colour. The grid should remain legible when the colour study is removed.

## Canon (study these)

Bauhaus-Archiv teaching studies; Herbert Bayer typography; workshop objects and the MoMA Bauhaus collection.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Ink on warm stock; each coloured shape is decorative and carries no small text. The layout collapses into a single reading column on phones.

## Signature moves

1. Compose with the grid rather than decorate it
2. Give a functional numeral real scale
3. Keep geometric masses balanced but asymmetric
4. Make the workshop index usable
