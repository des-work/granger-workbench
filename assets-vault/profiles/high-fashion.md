# High Fashion  (design profile: `high-fashion`)

**Era:** canon 1930s–90s editorial; evergreen wherever luxury speaks

*Editorial luxury: ivory field, ink-black Didone serifs at huge scale contrast, hairline rules, champagne accents and aggressive whitespace. The layout behaves like a magazine spread — photography and type carry everything.*

## Origins

The 20th-century editorial canon: Vogue and Harper's Bazaar under Brodovitch ('Astonish me'), Didot/Bodoni mastheads, 90s minimal luxury branding, runway invitations printed on paper heavier than the message. Luxury speaks quietly because it doesn't need to persuade.

## Composition & space

Whitespace is the luxury good — spend it extravagantly. One dominant element per spread, extreme scale hierarchy (a 140pt headline against a 9pt caption), rigid alignment used to stage dramatic asymmetry. Margins are wide because the content can afford them.

## Proportion & measure

Didone display reaches 160px against 18px reading text; captions remain legible. One image or original fashion plate dominates the spread. Off-centre crops, folios and hairline dividers replace decorative cards.

## Color logic

Near-absence: ivory field, ink black, and at most ONE guest — champagne metal or a single seasonal accent, never both. Black is the color; everything else is lighting. Saturation anywhere reads as discount.

## Light

Two modes, both deliberate: sculpted single-key studio light, or brutal noon sun with knife-edge shadows. Always high contrast, never golden-hour cozy — light is casting judgment, not comfort.

## Typography

Didone display — razor thick/thin contrast — letter-spaced caps for mastheads, refined serif for body, italic for whispered captions. Type is allowed to kiss or overlap the photography; scale contrast does the work decoration would.

## Materiality & finish

Uncoated stock, matte laminate, letterpress bite, foil applied with restraint. Surfaces are paper, not screens; there is no digital gloss, bevel or glow — the only shine permitted arrives by foil or silk.

## Motion & rhythm

Slow and editorial: long crossfades, masked reveals that behave like a page turning, text that rises a line at a time. Restraint is confidence; anything bouncy is beneath it.

## Imagery

Photography IS the design — full-bleed portraiture, controlled studio light or brutal noon sun, styled to the millimeter. Art direction replaces ornament; if the image is weak no layout will save the page. The portable starter uses an original vector fashion plate as a licensed local fallback; replace it with owned campaign photography when available. A small decorative icon is never a campaign image.

## Voice (copy & tone)

The declarative whisper: short verdicts without hedging — 'The coat. Nothing else.' French loanwords permitted, exclamation points never. Copy behaves like it was expensive to print, because it was.

## Sound

Near-silence: room tone, a heel on marble, one held cello note. If music, spare piano or cold electronica kept low — the audio equivalent of whitespace.

## Where it lives

Mastheads and campaign spreads, lookbooks, boutique windows, runway invitations, perfume boxes — objects whose job is to be desired before they are read.

## Kinship (family tree)

Parent of every 'quiet luxury' wave; cousin of `editorial` (shares the typographic discipline, adds glamour and cruelty); opposite of `preppy-2000s` (same money, opposite volume). Don't confuse with `art-deco` — deco decorates its luxury, fashion abstains from decoration entirely.

## Hierarchy of power

Imagery > typography > whitespace > color. Color is barely on the team — it's allowed to hold the coat while the photograph and the Didone do the talking.

## Canon (study these)

Study: Brodovitch's Harper's Bazaar spreads, the Vogue Didot masthead, Cassandre's YSL monogram, Phoebe Philo-era Céline lookbooks, Comme des Garçons invitations, any September-issue cover wall.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Ink on ivory sits near 17:1 — AAA without trying. Dark mode inverts gracefully to ivory-on-ink (fashion always loved a black page). Champagne is display-only — too soft for small text on ivory; spend it on numerals and rules. Letter-space caps only, never body text.

## Signature moves

1. One full-bleed image + one Didone headline — nothing else on the page
2. Letter-space the caps until they can breathe
3. Permit exactly one ornament: a hairline rule with a centered diamond
4. Cut every second element; then cut once more

## Components (this vault)

- **tokens/style**: `high-fashion` (`assets kit high-fashion` for a full stylesheet)
- **palette**: `runway-ivory` — #f7f4ee #ffffff #141414 #8e7747 #b4a284 #6b665c #d8d2c5

- **textures (native)**: paper
- **gloss**: none: matte paper only; if depth is needed use a 1px ink rule or a paper grain, never a shadow stack
- **shapes**: hairline-frame, editorial-rule
- **patterns**: —
- **techniques (fx)**: mask-fade-reveal, text-line-reveal, dropcap-initial-letter, text-balance-wrap

**Rules:** DO give one campaign image or original fashion plate real scale, set Didone display against legible reading text, use precise folios and cut surplus elements. DO NOT add gradient type, rounded cards, glow or decorative UI chrome.

**You've lost the plot when:** It collapses into mall-brand the instant there are two accent colors, a rounded corner, a gradient, or a crowded spread. If every element survived the edit, the edit didn't happen.
