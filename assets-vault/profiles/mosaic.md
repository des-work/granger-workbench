# Mosaic  (design profile: `mosaic`)

**Era:** antiquity → Gaudí c. 1900s → terrazzo-print revival 2018+

*Tessellation as a design language: terrazzo chips, Gaudí trencadís crackle, stained-glass panes with visible grout. Glazed jewel fragments (teal, terracotta, ochre, moss) on warm plaster; surfaces are fields of pieces, not solids.*

## Origins

Five thousand years of putting small pieces together: Roman and Byzantine tesserae, Islamic zellige geometry, Venetian terrazzo floors, Gaudí smashing tiles for Park Güell's trencadís (1900-14), cathedral stained glass — and the 2018+ terrazzo-print revival that put chips on everything again. Wholeness built from fragments.

## Composition & space

Surfaces are FIELDS OF PIECES: large forms emerge from small units, the way a face emerges from tesserae. Silhouettes stay bold and simple because the interior is busy. Borders are bands of tiles; the underlying grid is irregular and handmade, but the overall shape is architectural.

## Proportion & measure

Chips run 2-6% of the composition's width, smaller toward edges and detail zones. Grout gaps hold steady at 8-12% of chip size — consistency of gap is what sells the craft. Borders are one chip-row thick; silhouettes claim 60%+ of the frame so fragmentation never costs legibility.

## Color logic

Glazed jewel fragments — teal, terracotta, ochre, moss — distributed so no zone monopolizes a hue, on warm plaster/grout that is a REAL working color, not background. The cardinal rule: color changes happen BETWEEN pieces, never within one. A gradient inside a chip breaks the spell.

## Light

Raking light that catches grout ridges and glaze edges; sun through stained glass throwing colored patches on plaster. Every fragment glints on its own schedule — uniform studio lighting flattens the craft out of it.

## Typography

Inscriptional — engraved romans (Cinzel) that feel chiseled beside stone, or at display sizes letterforms literally assembled from tiles. Body text stays a quiet serif; the surfaces are the ornament, the text is the plaque.

## Materiality & finish

Fired glaze, stone chip, grout ridge, cool plaster. Each piece catches light slightly differently — a subtle per-fragment variance in tone and sheen is what makes it read as handmade rather than wallpaper.

## Motion & rhythm

Assembly and settling: pieces arrive staggered and click into place; transitions shatter and recompose kaleidoscopically. The rhythm is many small events composing one large one — never a single smooth sweep.

## Imagery

Architectural detail photography — courtyards, fountains, domes — and macro texture shots where grout lines are visible. Crafted objects over lifestyle; hands laying tiles over faces smiling.

## Voice (copy & tone)

Artisanal and patient: the copy talks about craft, provenance and hours — 'cut, placed and grouted by hand.' Metaphors of many-making-one. It never rushes and never brags in percentages.

## Sound

Ceramic clinks, stone on stone, courtyard reverb, Spanish guitar or oud. Rhythm built from many small hits — mallets, handclaps — the percussion equivalent of tesserae.

## Where it lives

Courtyards, fountains, floors you photograph your shoes on; spa and hotel identities, artisan food packaging, cultural institutions, anywhere craft is the promise.

## Kinship (family tree)

The ancestor tradition behind the terrazzo print trend; cousin of `jewel` (shares the gem tones, adds fragmentation); kindred spirit of `organic-tech` (both handmade-feeling — mosaic hard, organic soft). Don't confuse with pixel art: pixels obey a grid, tesserae follow the form.

## Hierarchy of power

Silhouette > fragment rhythm > palette distribution > grout color > type. If the outline reads from across the room and the pieces read up close, it's working at both distances — that's the whole trick.

## Canon (study these)

Study: San Vitale's mosaics in Ravenna, the zellige of Fez, Gaudí's serpentine bench at Park Güell, Venetian palazzo terrazzo, the Palau de la Música ceiling, and the 2018+ terrazzo-print packaging wave.

## Adaptation (modes & accessibility)

Text never sits on tesserae — give it a plaster plaque or a solid pane. Teal and terracotta clear 3:1 on plaster for large elements; body ink stays near-black. Dark mode is a night courtyard: charcoal grout, slate plaster, glazes slightly muted. Reduced motion: the assemble becomes a crossfade — drop the stagger.

## Signature moves

1. Build one bold silhouette out of many small chips
2. Let the grout be a visible, working color — not background
3. Border every panel with a tessera band
4. Vary each fragment's tone a few percent so it reads handmade

**Rules:** DO break big surfaces into visible pieces with grout gaps, vary fragment sizes. DON'T use seamless gradients — color changes happen BETWEEN pieces, not within them.

**You've lost the plot when:** Broken by seamless blends, perfectly regular grids, or grout that matches the tiles — if the pieces stop being visible as pieces, you've made a gradient with extra steps.
